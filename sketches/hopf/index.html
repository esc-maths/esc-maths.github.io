Hopf Fibration Visualization

What is a Hopf fibration?

The Hopf fibration is a way of connecting higher-dimensional spaces to our familiar 3D world. By using stereographic projection, these abstract fibers can be represented as smooth, interwoven circles in three dimensions.

It is a remarkable construction in mathematics that provides a mapping from the 3-sphere \(S^3\) to the 2-sphere \(S^2\) such that the preimage of each point in \(S^2\) is a circle in \(S^3\). This visualization project demonstrates Hopf fibers using stereographic projection into \(\mathbb{R}^3\).

The 3-Sphere \(S^3\)

The 3-sphere is defined as
\[ S^3 = \left\{ (x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4) \in \mathbb{R}^4 \,\bigg|\, x_1^2 + x_2^2 + x_3^2 + x_4^2 = 1 \right\}. \]
A point on \(S^3\) can also be represented using complex coordinates \((z_1, z_2) \in \mathbb{C}^2\) such that
\[ z_1 = x_1 + i x_2, \quad z_2 = x_3 + i x_4, \quad |z_1|^2 + |z_2|^2 = 1. \]

Spinor Representation

Given a point \(n = (x, y, z)\) on \(S^2\), we can associate a spinor in \(\mathbb{C}^2\) as follows: \[ z_1 = \sqrt{\frac{1+n_z}{2}}, \quad z_2 = \frac{x + i y}{2 z_1}. \] This representation is useful for computing Hopf fibers and their rotation phases.

Hopf Fiber Parametrization

A Hopf fiber corresponding to a point on \(S^2\) can be parametrized as \[ (z_1 e^{i \theta}, z_2 e^{i \theta}), \quad \theta \in [0, 2\pi). \] The points in \(\mathbb{R}^3\) after stereographic projection are \[ \mathbf{p} = \left( \frac{x_1}{1 - x_4}, \frac{x_2}{1 - x_4}, \frac{x_3}{1 - x_4} \right). \]

Stereographic Projection

Stereographic projection from \(S^3 \subset \mathbb{R}^4\) to \(\mathbb{R}^3\) is defined by
\[ \pi: S^3 \setminus \{N\} \to \mathbb{R}^3, \quad \pi(x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4) = \left( \frac{x_1}{1 - x_4}, \frac{x_2}{1 - x_4}, \frac{x_3}{1 - x_4} \right), \]
where \(N = (0,0,0,1)\) is the north pole of \(S^3\). This maps circles (Hopf fibers) in \(S^3\) to circles in \(\mathbb{R}^3\).

Hopf Fibration Visualization

In the project, the 3D visualization is constructed as follows:
  1. Sample points \(n \in S^2\) as fiber directions.
  2. Convert \(n\) into a spinor \((z_1, z_2)\).
  3. Rotate the phase \(\theta \in [0, 2\pi)\) to generate points on the fiber.
  4. Apply stereographic projection to map points from \(S^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3\).
  5. Render the fibers using Three.js.

Patterns and Examples

Some patterns that can be visualized include:
  • Tropical pattern: Points distributed along the equator and tropics of \(S^2\).
  • Spiral pattern: Points along a spiral from north to south pole.
  • Icosahedral pattern: Points at the vertices of an icosahedron projected onto \(S^2\).
  • Longitude bands: Points along meridians for a uniform grid.

How is this rendered?

To turn the mathematics into something visible, this project translates Hopf fibers into 3D geometry and renders them with Three.js. The library takes care of the heavy lifting of real-time 3D graphics, including lighting, and shading, so the focus can stay on the geometry itself.

Thanks to Three.js, this project can:
  • Render the intricate linked circles of the fibration smoothly in real time.
  • Let you rotate, zoom, and explore the fibers interactively in your browser.
  • Use color, depth, and motion to highlight patterns that are hard to see on paper.
In this way, abstract mathematics becomes something you can explore visually and intuitively.
