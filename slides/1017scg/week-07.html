Finding the Equation of a Line

Through 2 points, we can always draw a straight line!



Finding the Equation of a Line

Consider the following two points $(x_1,y_1)\,$ and $\,(x_2,y_2)$



Finding the Equation of a Line

👉 Consider $(x_1,y_1)$ and any other point $\,(x,y)$



Finding the Equation of a Line

👉 $\;m = \dfrac{y-y_1}{x-x_1}\;\;$ and $\;\;m = \dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}$

$\Ra\; \dfrac{y-y_1}{x-x_1}$ $=$ $\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}$
$\Ra\; y-y_1 $ $=$ $\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\left(x-x_1\right)$
$\Ra \;y-y_1 $ $=$ $m \left(x-x_1\right)$



✨ The equation of a line ✨

$y-y_1 = m \left(x-x_1\right)\;$ with $\;m = \dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}$

Example 1

Find the equation of the line that has a gradient of 3 and passes through the point $(2,5).$

👉 $\;y-y_1 = m \left(x-x_1\right)$

\(y-5\) \(=\) \( 3(x-2)\)
\(y-5\) \(=\) \(3x-6\)
\(y-5+5\) \(=\) \( 3x-6+5\)
\(y\) \(=\) \( 3x-1 \)

Example 2

Find the equation of the line that has a gradient of 4 and passes through the point $(-3,-7).$

👉 $\;y-y_1 = m \left(x-x_1\right)$

\(y-(-7)\) \(=\) \( 4(x-(-3))\)
\(y+7\) \(=\) \(4(x+3)\)
\(y+7\) \(=\) \( 4x+12\)
\(y+7-7\) \(=\) \( 4x+12 -7 \)
\(y\) \(=\) \( 4x+5 \)
