Question

The temperature ($T$) of a cup of tea changes according to the following equation

\( \ds\frac{dT}{dt} = -k\left(T-T_{\text{amb}}\right), \)

where $T_{\text{amb}}=25^\circ\text{C}$ is the ambient room temperature, and $k = 0.005\text{ second}^{-1}$ is the heat transfer coefficient.

  1. Solve the ODE to show that $T(t) = C_0 e^{-0.005t} + 25$.
  2. Calculate how long it will take for the tea to cool to $55^\circ\text{C}$ if it has an initial temperature of $90^\circ\text{C}.$


Solution Question 1. Solve differential equation.

The temperature satisfies \( \ds \frac{dT}{dt} = -k\left(T-T_{\text{amb}}\right). \) Separate the variables.

\( \ds \frac{1}{T-T_{\text{amb}}}\, dT = -k\, dt. \)

\( \Ra\ds \int \frac{1}{T-T_{\text{amb}}}\, dT = \ds - k \int dt \;\) \( \Ra \;\ds \ln |T-T_{\text{amb}}| = -kt + C_0. \)

Apply exponential function on both sides: \( \ds T-T_{\text{amb}} = C_0e^{-kt}. \)

Hence, the general solution is:

\( \ds T(t)=C_0e^{-kt}+T_{\text{amb}}\; \) or \(\;\ds T(t)=C_0e^{-0.005t}+25.\)



Solution Question 2. What is the time to cool down from $90^\circ\text{C}$ to $55^\circ\text{C}$?

Initial temperature is \( T(0)=90^\circ\text{C}. \) Substitute into \( T(t)=C_0e^{-kt}+ T_{\text{amb}} . \)

\( 90 = C_0+T_{\text{amb}} \) \( \Rightarrow C_0 = 90 - T_{\text{amb}} \) \( = 90 - 25 \) \( = 65. \)

Thus \(\; \ds T(t)=65e^{-0.005t}+ 25. \)

We know that \( T_{\text{amb}} = 25^\circ\text{C}. \) Set \( T(t)=55^\circ\text{C}. \)

\( \Ra\; 55 = 65e^{-0.005t}+25\; \) \(\; \Ra \) \(\; \ds \frac{6}{13} = \) \( \ds \frac{30}{65} = e^{-0.005t}\qquad \)

\( \Ra \ds \ln\!\left(\frac{6}{13}\right) = -0.005t \) \(\; \Ra\; \ds t = \frac{\ln\!\left(\frac{6}{13}\right)}{-0.005} \) \( \approx 155 \text{ seconds}. \)
