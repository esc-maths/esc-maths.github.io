Question 1

Find $\;\ds \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\, dx$


Question 2

Compute $\;\ds \int\frac{x+1}{x(x+2)}\, dx$



Solution Question 1

Use the trigonometric substitution \( x = \sin\theta \). Then \( dx = \cos\theta\, d\theta \) and

\( \sqrt{1-x^2} = \sqrt{1-\sin^2\theta} \) \( = \cos\theta. \)

\( \ds \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^2}} \) \( = \ds \int \frac{\cos\theta}{\cos\theta}\, d\theta \) \( = \ds \int d\theta \) \( \ds = \theta + C \qquad \qquad \qquad \)

\( \ds = \sin^{-1}(x) + C \) \( \ds = \arcsin(x) + C. \)

Alternatively, recall that \( \ds \frac{d}{dx}(\sin^{-1}x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}. \) Thus

\( \ds \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^2}} \) \( = \ds \sin^{-1}(x) + C. \)



Solution Question 2

We begin by expressing the integrand using partial fractions.

\( \ds \frac{x+1}{x(x+2)} = \frac{A}{x} + \frac{B}{x+2}. \)

Comparing coefficients gives the system: \(A+B=1,\; 2A=1.\)

This implies \( A = \dfrac12 \) and \( B = \dfrac12 \).

Then \( \ds \int \frac{x+1}{x(x+2)}\,dx \) \( = \ds \frac12 \int \frac{1}{x}dx + \frac{1}{2}\int \frac{1}{x+2}\,dx\qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \quad \)

\( \qquad \qquad \quad \ds = \frac12 \ln|x| + \frac12 \ln|x+2| + C \) \( \ds = \frac12 \ln|x(x+2)| + C. \)
