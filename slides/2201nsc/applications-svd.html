Applications of the SVD

SVD Recall & Matrix Factorisation

Any matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ can be written as $A = U \Sigma V^T$, where $U \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times m}$ and $V \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ are orthogonal matrices, and $\Sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ contains singular values $\sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_r > 0$.

$$ A = \begin{pmatrix} | & | & & | \\ u_1 & u_2 & \dots & u_m \\ | & | & & | \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \sigma_1 & & \\ & \sigma_2 & \\ & & \ddots \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} - & v_1^T & - \\ - & v_2^T & - \\ & \vdots & \end{pmatrix} = \sum_{i=1}^{r} \sigma_i u_i v_i^T $$
The SVD decomposes $A$ into a linear combination of rank-1 outer product matrices.

The SVD as an Outer Product Expansion

The expansion $A = \sum_{i=1}^{r} \sigma_i u_i v_i^T$ represents $A$ as a weighted sum of outer products $u_i v_i^T$:

$$ u_i v_i^T = \begin{pmatrix} u_{1i} \\ u_{2i} \\ \vdots \\ u_{mi} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} v_{1i} & v_{2i} & \dots & v_{ni} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} u_{1i}v_{1i} & u_{1i}v_{2i} & \dots & u_{1i}v_{ni} \\ u_{2i}v_{1i} & u_{2i}v_{2i} & \dots & u_{2i}v_{ni} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ u_{mi}v_{1i} & u_{mi}v_{2i} & \dots & u_{mi}v_{ni} \end{pmatrix} $$
  • Orthonormality of $U$ and $V$ implies $\|u_i\| = \|v_i\| = 1$.
  • Bounded element magnitude: $|u_{ji}| \le 1$ and $|v_{ki}| \le 1$.
  • Sum of squared entries over each term satisfies $\sum_{j,k} (u_{ji} v_{ki})^2 = 1$.

Low-Rank Approximation & Data Truncation

Because singular values are ordered ($\sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_r > 0$), term significance drops rapidly:

$$ A_k = \sum_{i=1}^{k} \sigma_i u_i v_i^T \quad (k < r) $$

Data Savings Comparison:

Original Storage

$m \times n$ entries

Truncated $k$-rank SVD

$k \times (m + n + 1)$ entries


Application: SVD Image Compression

Workflow for compressing image data using SVD truncation:

  1. Matrix Representation: Convert image pixels into intensity matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$.
  2. Factorisation: Compute full SVD: $A = U \Sigma V^T$.
  3. Rank Culling: Eliminate zero singular values to determine exact rank $r$.
  4. Compression: Discard smallest $\sigma_i$ values along with corresponding columns of $U$ and rows of $V^T$.
Reduces parameter storage from $m \times n$ down to $k \times (m + n + 1)$ while preserving dominant visual structure.

Geometric Interpretation: Action on Unit Circle

Consider transformation of unit circle points $x \in \mathbb{R}^2$ ($||x|| = 1$) under matrix $A$:

$$ A = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 4 & 2 \end{pmatrix} $$

Maps unit circle into an ellipse in $\mathbb{R}^2$.

Key Questions:

  • In which direction is data stretched most?
  • In which direction is data stretched least?

Deriving Maximum and Minimum Stretch

For $\|x\| = 1$, distance from origin is $\|Ax\| = \sqrt{x^T A^T A x}$. Since $A^T A$ is symmetric positive-definite ($V D V^T$):

$$ x = c_1 v_1 + c_2 v_2 = V c \quad \text{with} \quad c_1^2 + c_2^2 = 1 $$
$$ \|Ax\| = \sqrt{(c^T V^T)(V D V^T)(V c)} = \sqrt{c^T D c} = \sqrt{\lambda_1 c_1^2 + \lambda_2 c_2^2} $$

Maximum Stretch

$c_1 = 1, c_2 = 0 \implies x = v_1$

Length $\|Ax\| = \sqrt{\lambda_1} = \sigma_1$

Minimum Stretch

$c_1 = 0, c_2 = 1 \implies x = v_2$

Length $\|Ax\| = \sqrt{\lambda_2} = \sigma_2$


Principal Directions & Matrix $V$

  • The eigenvectors of $A^T A$ define orthogonal directions of principal stretching.
  • Higher Dimensions: Columns of $V$ sequentially order axes from greatest stretching to lowest stretching.
  • These principal directions correspond directly to the rows of $V^T$ in $A = U \Sigma V^T$.
$V$ identifies the domain orientation vectors, while $\Sigma$ scales their transformed magnitudes.

Directions of Maximum Variance in Data

For a dataset $X \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times 2}$ containing $m$ observations:

$$ X = \begin{pmatrix} x_1 & y_1 \\ x_2 & y_2 \\ \vdots & \vdots \\ x_m & y_m \end{pmatrix} $$

Principal Axes of Variance:

Obtained directly from rows of $V^T$ in SVD expansion $X = U \Sigma V^T$.

Conclusion: SVD automatically provides Principal Component Analysis (PCA) directions without forming explicit covariance matrices.
